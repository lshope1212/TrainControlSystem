## 2. Fix the CTC route-planning algorithm

The current CTC uses shortest-path routing based primarily on connectivity.

Investigate:

- `src/CTC/CTC.Core/Dispatching/RouteManager.cs`
- `src/CTC/CTC.Core/Scheduling/ScheduleTemplateConverter.cs`
- `src/CTC/CTC.Core/Scheduling/RouteGeometry.cs`

### Current problems

1. The route planner does not fully enforce the track's direction of travel.
2. It does not properly consider the stem and branches of switches.
3. It prevents revisiting any block.
4. It can construct a physically invalid route that is later rejected by the Track Controller.

### Required changes

Implement a general-purpose topology-aware route planner.

The planner must:

- Respect directed track travel.
- Respect switch geometry.
- Never route from one switch leg directly to another without passing through the stem.
- Allow valid repeated block visits when the network requires them.
- Avoid immediate reversals.
- Prevent infinite loops.
- Select a valid route to the destination.
- Work for Blue, Red, and Green without line-specific hardcoding.
- Support the CTC Schedule Builder and dispatcher rerouting.

I recommend investigating a graph-search state that includes both the current block and the block from which the train entered.

For example:

`RoutingState(PreviousBlockId, CurrentBlockId)`

This allows a train to revisit a physical block from a different approach while preventing endless exploration of identical routing states.

Choose an appropriate shortest-valid-path algorithm, such as BFS over directed routing states, or another approach justified by the actual topology.

Reuse existing track-direction and switch-geometry logic where possible so CTC and Track Controller agree on which connections are legal.

### Schedule Builder behavior

The dispatcher must be able to:

1. Select Green Line.
2. Create a schedule for Train 001.
3. Select GREEN-63 as the starting block.
4. Enter an arrival time at Edgebrook, GREEN-9.
5. Queue the schedule successfully.

The Schedule Builder must automatically fill in the valid route.

The dispatcher should NOT need to manually enter all 117 block visits or add arbitrary intermediate timed stops to force the correct route.

Preserve the ability to create schedules with multiple timed waypoints.

## 3. Support repeated blocks throughout CTC and Track Model

The full route revisits GREEN-77 through GREEN-85.

A repeated physical block is not necessarily an invalid route. The distinction is that the train enters it from a different predecessor and at a different point in its journey.

Review how route positions are represented in:

- `DispatchedTrainState`
- `ScheduledTrain`
- `ScheduledRouteBlock`
- `MovementPlanner`
- `TrainLocationTracker`
- `TrainDispatchCoordinator`
- `TrackService.PositionTracking.cs`
- `TrackTopology.cs`

### Required behavior

The train's progress must be represented by its current occurrence or index in the ordered route, not just a unique block ID.

For example, the first visit to GREEN-85 and the second visit to GREEN-85 must be distinguishable.

Verify that:

- Physical dispatch accepts the valid full route.
- Track Model position tracking supports repeated blocks in the planned route.
- Cumulative position remains continuous and does not reset when a block is revisited.
- CTC location inference advances along the correct route occurrence.
- An occupancy transition does not accidentally move the train to the wrong occurrence of a repeated block.
- Route updates and movement-plan indices remain valid.
- The train is not incorrectly marked as having completed its route on the first visit to a repeated block.

Preserve the existing principle that the CTC infers train identity from anonymous block occupancy and known routes.

Do not introduce train IDs into Track Controller block-occupancy reports just to simplify tracking.

When occupancy information is ambiguous, retain conservative behavior rather than guessing.

## 4. Fix movement authority across repeated switches and route segments

**This is a critical part of the task. Do not stop after fixing route generation.**

The Track Controller currently rejects a movement request that revisits the same block.

See:

`src/TrackController/TrackController.Core/Services/TrackControllerService.Requests.cs`

The existing `SubmitMovement()` validation includes a distinct-block check.

This is reasonable for a single protected movement request, even though the train's entire journey can revisit blocks.

Additionally, the recently added automatic switch-alignment functionality derives switch demands from active movement requests.

If one request includes both visits to switch 77 or switch 85, the system may demand two different positions from the same switch simultaneously.

### Required solution

Design and implement a safe way to execute the full route using appropriate movement-authority segments.

**Preferred approach: Keep the full route as the train's planned journey, but issue bounded movement requests over appropriate portions of it.**

These requests should not contain repeated block IDs or simultaneous conflicting switch alignments.

The system must automatically progress between these segments as the train advances.

Do not require the dispatcher to manually issue new movement requests at each junction.

### Requirements for segmentation

1. Each movement request covers a contiguous, physically valid portion of the train's route.
2. Each request contains enough route context for the Track Controller to safely evaluate it.
3. A request cannot demand two incompatible positions for the same switch.
4. Switch positions must be commanded and confirmed before the train is authorized to traverse them.
5. The CTC must automatically prepare or issue further movement requests as the train progresses.
6. The train must eventually obtain authority through the entire route.
7. Movement authority must never jump forward or extend implicitly just because a train enters another block.

### Preserve existing authority safety

The current system uses a fixed authority endpoint and block-to-block handoff mechanism.

Preserve its safety guarantees.

In particular:

- A renewal of an existing request must not extend its authority.
- A staged successor must not exceed its predecessor's authorized endpoint or speed.
- A new movement segment that extends beyond an earlier endpoint must use an explicit new request, with a new request ID and full Track Controller evaluation.
- Do not bypass Track Controller approval.
- Do not grant optimistic authority while a new segment is being prepared.
- Do not allow stale or cancelled requests to regain authority.
- Preserve safe braking behavior if new authority is delayed or withheld.
- Avoid unnecessary zero-authority gaps when safe, without weakening the existing handoff rules.

Choose segment boundaries carefully, particularly near switch protection zones.

A train should not be allowed to move into a switch protection zone and then become permanently unable to change the switch needed for its subsequent route.

The system should prepare the required alignment early enough to avoid this deadlock where safely possible.

### Specific Green Line scenario

The train must successfully:

1. Cross switch 77 on its Normal branch.
2. Cross switch 85 on its Normal branch.
3. Travel around the loop to GREEN-100.
4. Have switch 85 safely transition to Reverse for the second traversal.
5. Continue back through GREEN-85 and GREEN-84.
6. Have switch 77 safely transition to Reverse for the second traversal.
7. Continue to GREEN-101 and onward.

Pay special attention to reservation ownership, approach locking, and the occupancy protection zones around these switches.

If the switch cannot safely move, the authority must remain restricted and the movement should resume automatically when conditions permit.
