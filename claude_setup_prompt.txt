# Claude Code Prompt — Fix Automatic Switch Alignment and Route Authorization

## Objective

Investigate and fix the automatic switch alignment problem in our TrainControlSystem repository.

The system currently dispatches and moves trains successfully when their route follows the initial positions of the switches. However, when a route requires a switch to change position, the Track Controller accepts the movement request but indefinitely withholds authority.

**Goal: A train's planned route should automatically cause the necessary switches to be aligned safely, with the Track Controller verifying actual switch feedback before granting authority.**

Implement this as a proper production integration fix, not a workaround.

## 1. Reproduce and understand the problem

The system uses C#/.NET, WPF, MVVM, named-pipe communication, and separate CTC, Track Controller, Track Model, Train Controller, and Train Model modules.

We have recently completed much of the physical dispatch and movement integration.

### Reproduction scenario

On the Blue Line:

- Train 001 starts at block 1.
- The scheduled destination is Station C at block 15.
- The route follows blocks 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15.
- Switch 5 initially reports Normal.
- This route requires switch 5 to be Reverse, connecting the stem at block 4 toward block 11.

The train successfully dispatches into the Track Model, and the movement request is delivered to the Track Controller.

The CTC displays a message similar to:

"Train 001 movement request for blocks 1 to 15 (500 m): accepted, but authority withheld: Waiting for switch 5 alignment feedback; this request needs 11 (verify entry and alignment)."

Consequently, the train receives zero movement authority and remains stopped.

The reverse scenario also occurs: routes that require Normal alignment may fail when the switch initially lies Reverse.

### Existing integration test workaround

Examine:

`tests/Integration.Tests/SignalledRouteIntegrationTests.cs`

The test `Train001_ToStationC_ClearsSignal11_IsAuthorized_AndMoves` currently initializes switch 5 to Reverse before starting the test.

The comment explicitly describes the limitation that prevents the Track Controller from throwing the switch live.

**This workaround must no longer be necessary after the fix.**

## 3. Known issues to investigate and resolve

### Issue A — No automatic switch position selection

The CTC already supports explicit switch requests through:

`CTCService.SetSwitchPositionAsync()`

However, switch requests are separate from movement requests.

The Track Controller's evaluation logic also appears to use the reported switch position whenever no desired switch position has been specified.

As a result, the movement request specifies the desired route but does not cause the required switch change.

**Required fix:**

Implement automatic switch alignment as part of normal movement preparation.

The system must determine which switch positions a route requires based on the actual topology, including:

- The block from which the train enters the switch.
- The block through which it must exit.
- The switch's stem, Normal branch, and Reverse branch.
- Whether the route is physically valid.

For Blue Line block 5, the route 4 → 5 → 11 requires Reverse.

Choose the best architectural solution after inspecting the code. This might involve the CTC proactively requesting switch alignment or the Track Controller deriving required positions from accepted movement requests.

Prefer reusing existing interfaces and controls instead of creating a redundant switch-control mechanism.

The Track Controller must remain responsible for enforcing safety and deciding whether switching is actually permitted.

Do not let the CTC directly manipulate the Track Model's switch position.

### Issue B — Missing CTC reservation feedback

Investigate the switch safety logic in:

`TrackControllerService.Requests.cs`

Particularly:

`GetSwitchProtectionReason()`

The current implementation requires each block in the switch's protection zone to have:

`IsReservedByCtc == false`

However, the Track Model's `CreateBlockState()` does not currently populate `IsReservedByCtc`.

Therefore, reservation feedback is null and the Track Controller treats the protection zone as unsafe.

The PLC similarly uses `reserved_known` and `reserved`.

**Required fix:**

Implement a proper, truthful means of determining whether the switch protection zone is reserved by CTC.

Investigate whether reservation ownership already exists somewhere in the system before introducing new state or messages.

If it does not exist, implement the minimum complete reservation-tracking integration necessary to support safe automatic switch operation.

Requirements:

- Do not simply hardcode `IsReservedByCtc = false`.
- Do not bypass the reservation check.
- Do not treat unknown reservation state as proof that the zone is unreserved.
- Clearly define what constitutes a reservation, who owns it, and when it is created or released.
- Prevent conflicting train movements or switch changes through a protected zone.
- Ensure that stale or unavailable reservation information results in a safe restriction.
- Keep the feedback synchronized through the existing communication architecture.

Be careful to distinguish a movement request that is awaiting switch alignment from an already granted movement authority. A pending route must not unnecessarily deadlock itself by being treated as an incompatible reservation.

### Issue C — Command and feedback synchronization

Once the required switch position is selected and approved:

- The Track Controller should issue the command through the existing Track Model command pipeline.
- The Track Model should change the switch only when physically safe.
- The Track Model must publish the new actual switch position.
- The Track Controller must verify that the reported position matches the required position.
- Authority must remain withheld until the switch alignment is confirmed.

Acknowledging a switch request is not equivalent to confirming that the switch moved.

If a command is rejected, blocked, or never confirmed, the system must clearly report why and must not grant unsafe authority.

Verify that repeated command publication, evaluation cycles, and asynchronous feedback cannot cause the system to oscillate, incorrectly reset switch positions, or generate conflicting commands.

## 4. Support automatic alignment during the entire journey

This functionality must not work only at initial dispatch.

A train may have multiple junctions along its route. A dispatcher may also change the destination after the train has departed.

The implementation should handle:

- An initial route that requires a switch change.
- Several switches requiring different alignments.
- A train approaching a switch that is already correctly aligned.
- A train approaching a switch that needs to change alignment.
- A route update requiring a different switch position.
- A switch that cannot currently be moved because its protection zone is occupied, reserved, faulted, or otherwise unsafe.
- Multiple trains with potentially conflicting movement requests.

A switch change must happen only when safe.

If a switch cannot be aligned, withhold the necessary authority and clearly report the reason. Reevaluate when conditions change so that the train can continue automatically once the route becomes safe.

Do not throw a switch beneath a train or within an unsafe protection zone.

Preserve authority continuity and the existing movement-request handoff mechanism.

## 5. Preserve the current safety architecture

This project is intended to realistically simulate railway operations.

Therefore:

- The CTC requests movement but never grants the final safety authority.
- The Track Controller remains the final authority for movement and switch control.
- The Track Model represents the actual physical switch state.
- Reported switch alignment must be verified, not assumed.
- Missing or stale feedback must remain fail-safe.
- An occupied or protected switch must not be thrown.
- The PLC-based hardware mode must remain supported.
- Software and PLC modes must enforce equivalent safety requirements.
- Cancellation, route changes, watchdog expiration, and loss of communication must not leave unsafe switch commands or authorizations active.

Do not solve this by removing interlocks, forcing signals Green, granting authority optimistically, or modifying the Train Controller to ignore zero authority.
